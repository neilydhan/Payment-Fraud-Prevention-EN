# Preface

(Draft)

This book began as an article about 3DS. While writing it, I found that the authentication protocol on its own tells only part of the story. How attackers avoid authentication, how card networks measure merchants, how recurring billing behaves, how to respond when something breaks: each piece connects to several others. Businesses that sell across markets need the whole map.

The book has four parts:

1. Part I covers the foundations: who takes part in a card payment, how a transaction flows from end to end, and the main types of fraud.
2. Part II covers authentication and card network rules: what 3DS can and cannot do, how card networks monitor merchants, and what regulators require in different markets.
3. Part III covers how to build a fraud program: risk signals, rules and models, and specific areas such as card setup, recurring billing, early fraud warnings and disputes.
4. Part IV covers operations: metrics, monitoring, incident response, and what AI changes.

Every chapter aims for two things. Facts should be traceable to public sources, and conclusions should translate into actions a business can take.

The views in this book are those of the author alone and do not represent any employer or organization. Nothing in it is risk, compliance or legal advice for any specific situation.
